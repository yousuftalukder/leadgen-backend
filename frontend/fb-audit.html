How Community Audit works — read this first
The big picture. Edgelead is one app with two halves. The Instagram half (Lead Finder, Performance Audit, Competitor Intel) works on accounts. The Facebook half is these four pages, and they all run on one engine called fb_community — an admin has to grant you that engine and you have to save an Apify token for it, or every Facebook page will just return "access denied". Everything on the Facebook side is about groups only, and public groups only. A private group is skipped on purpose, because reading it would need a logged-in session and this tool will not do that.

The idea

Discovery told you which rooms. The audit tells you what to actually do inside them.

It pulls a much deeper sample than discovery — by default around 120 posts per group across a 60-day window — and then takes the room apart. Every post is scored against that room's own median, so "1.8x" means 1.8 times better than a typical post in that specific group. Comparing a village group to a city group on raw likes would be meaningless; comparing each room to itself is not.

How you use this page

  1. Pick rooms from your saved list, or press Select top 10 by Room Value to take the best ones discovery found.
  2. Choose Combined or Individual mode (explained below).
  3. Set the days window and posts per group. Optionally turn on comment sampling — it is the expensive part, roughly 1.6× the cost.
  4. Press Run community audit. It shows live progress while scraping, then writes the narrative with AI and saves the report.
  5. Read the report, download it as PDF, then take the winning room over to Post Advisor to get drafts built on these numbers.

Combined or Individual — pick the question you are asking

Combined modeOne report covering every selected room, with a cross-room ranking and a rolled-up playbook. Answers: "which of my rooms deserve the effort?"
Individual modeA separate report per room, all produced in one job. Answers: "how do I win inside this one room?"

What the report tells you

  • Which format wins — photo vs text vs video vs link vs album vs poll, each scored as a multiple of that room's median. So you get statements like "photo posts run at 1.8x this room's median across 34 posts". It also names the format the room suppresses, so you know what to avoid.
  • Which intent wins — question, story, offer, event, discussion, recommendation request. In most local rooms, asking beats telling, and the number proves it.
  • Best time to post — an hour × day-of-week heatmap built from real performance, not guesswork.
  • Best length — short (under 40 words), medium (40–120), or long (120+).
  • Best opening pattern — how the winning posts start their first line.
  • Top 10 and bottom 10 posts, with links, so you can read what actually worked and what died.
  • What the room is shopping for — demand keywords and demand categories pulled from the posts where people are actively asking to buy.
  • Compliance flags — "this group bans promotion", or "posts go through an approval queue, so treat the timing heatmap as directional only".
  • Room health stats — posts per day, unique posters, median comments and reactions, and what share of posts come from admins.

Reports, sets and trends

Every run is saved in the Reports tab and can be reopened or exported to PDF at any time. If the rooms were saved as a set, you can re-run the exact same set later and the trend view will show you whether those rooms are getting livelier or dying off.

Cost control. Cost scales with rooms × posts per room, and comment sampling multiplies it by about 1.6. The estimate box updates live before you run anything — check it. Auditing 10 rooms at 120 posts each is 1,200 posts of scraping.
What this page is not. There is no Facebook Page audit anywhere in Edgelead — no follower growth, reach or post performance for your own or a competitor's business Page. The Facebook side only understands groups. Account-level performance reporting exists only for Instagram, on the IG Performance Audit and Competitor Intel pages.

Audit your rooms

Pull the posting history, index every post against that room's own median, and mine the text for unmet local demand. Raw likes lie because rooms differ in size — an index of 3.0 means three times that room's typical post, and that number is comparable across a 5k group and a 50k group.
