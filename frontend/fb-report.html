How the Facebook Page Report works — read this first
What this page is. Every other Facebook page in Edgelead works on groups. This one works on Pages — a business Page, yours or anyone's. Give it a Page URL and it pulls the profile and the posting history, takes both apart, scores the Page out of 100, and writes the report. Add a rival Page and you get a head-to-head as well. It runs on its own engine, fb_page, so an admin has to grant you that engine and a token has to be saved against it.

The idea

Raw likes are meaningless across pages of different sizes. Every post here is scored against that Page's own median for that month.

An index of 1.8x means the post did 1.8 times what a typical post on that Page does. That number is directly comparable between a 900-follower shop and a 900,000-follower brand, and it does not get distorted when a Page grows during the window.

How you use it

  1. Paste the Page URL or handle of the business you want reported on.
  2. Optionally paste one rival Page. Leave it empty for a solo report.
  3. Set how many posts and how far back to look. Check the estimate before you run.
  4. Press Generate report and watch the live progress.
  5. Read it, export it to PDF, and it stays in the Report vault tab for good.

Everything the report measures

Page Score /100Six weighted pillars — engagement per follower, cadence, consistency, conversation, amplification, profile completeness — with the points shown pillar by pillar so you can see where they went.
Profile completenessCategory, about, website, phone, email, address, hours, photos, CTA button, reviews. The cheapest wins on the whole page.
Cadence and consistencyPosts per week, median gap, longest silence, days since the last post, and a rhythm score that separates steady from bursty.
Format leaderboardPhoto, album, video, link, text and poll, each as a multiple of the Page's own median — including the format the audience suppresses.
Reaction sentimentThe full reaction mix. What share is a plain Like, what share took real effort, and whether anything is drawing angry or sad.
Conversation and amplificationComments per 100 reactions and shares per 100 reactions — the two ratios that actually predict reach.
Timing heatmapHour by day-of-week, built from real performance in local time rather than a generic "best time to post" chart.
Copy patternsCaption length bands, opening lines, questions, calls to action, offers, emoji and outbound links — each measured for lift.
MomentumMonth-by-month trajectory plus a newest-third against oldest-third comparison, so a slide shows up before it becomes a crisis.
Top and bottom postsThe ten best and ten worst with links, so you can read what worked instead of guessing.

Adding a rival

With a rival Page supplied you get a metric-by-metric table with an explicit winner on every row, the share of voice split, the formats and intents the rival uses that you do not, hashtags they own, their five best posts, and the gaps turned into dated actions.

Cost control. Cost scales with pages × posts per page. Two pages at 100 posts each is 200 posts of scraping. The estimate box updates live before you run anything.
Limits. Public Pages only. A personal profile or a group URL will be rejected — groups belong on the Community Audit page. Some fields depend on what the Page has chosen to publish: if follower count or the reaction breakdown is not returned, the report says so rather than inventing a number.
